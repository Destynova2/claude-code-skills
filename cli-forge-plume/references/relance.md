# Relances — Escalation Ladder and Humor Bank

A follow-up is not a repeat: each relance must be cheaper to answer than the
previous message, and ideally carry something new. Guilt and fake urgency are
banned at every rung.

## The ladder

**R0 — the original ask sets up the relance.** A dated ask ("pour vendredi ?")
or an options ask makes every later relance shorter and legitimate.

**R1 — neutral (email: +3 to 5 business days; chat: next morning).**
- One or two lines. Restate the ask in one sentence, never "as per my last
  email" / "comme indiqué dans mon précédent mail".
- Bring one new element if possible: a deadline that moved, a piece of info,
  a narrower question ("même juste un oui/non me suffit").
- No apology for following up. No humor yet: R1 must stay quotable in any
  context.

**R2 — light (a week after R1).**
- Still short. One humor mechanism allowed (see bank), placed after the ask
  or in a P.S.
- Reduce the ask again: propose a default ("sans retour, je pars sur
  l'option A lundi") when the user has authority to set one — this is a
  service, not a threat, so keep the tone flat.

**R3 — closing the loop (final).**
- Make silence an acceptable answer, and say so without drama: "si ce n'est
  plus d'actualité, dis-le-moi et je ferme le sujet."
- The numbered-options mechanism shines here (see bank).
- After R3, stop. New relances only if genuinely new information appears.

Three relances maximum on one ask. If three relances failed, the problem is
the ask or the channel, not the phrasing — suggest a channel switch (call,
corridor, another contact) instead of a fourth email.

## Timing norms per channel

| Channel | R1 | R2 | R3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email (external) | +3-5 business days | +1 week | +1-2 weeks |
| Email (internal) | +2-3 days | +3-4 days | +1 week |
| Chat (Slack/Teams) | next morning | +2 days, consider switching to email or a call | — |
| LinkedIn DM | +1 week | +2 weeks | usually skip |

Chat bumps stay minimal: a one-liner re-ask beats "up" or a lone "?", both of
which read as pressure.

## Humor mechanism bank

One mechanism per message, R2 or later, never in the ask sentence itself.

1. **Callback** — reuse a concrete detail from the previous exchange.
   *"Promis, ça prendra moins de temps que votre migration Postgres."*
   Strongest mechanism: it proves you listened. Zero risk if the detail is
   neutral.
2. **Self-deprecation légère** — the joke lands on you.
   *"Je commence à connaître ce mail par cœur, donc je le raccourcis :"*
3. **Options numérotées** — turn the answer into a multiple-choice.
   *"Réponse en un chiffre : 1 = ok pour jeudi, 2 = pas maintenant,
   3 = laisse tomber (et j'arrête de te relancer)."*
   Include the exit option: it is what makes the mechanism sympathique.
4. **Compteur honnête** — announce the ladder.
   *"Relance 2/3 — après, j'arrête, promis."*
5. **P.S. slot** — the body stays sober, the wink goes in the P.S.
   *"P.S. : mon record de relances est à trois. Aidez-moi à ne pas le
   battre."*
6. **L'absurde doux** — blame a neutral third thing, never a person.
   *"Je soupçonne mon premier mail d'être parti en vacances sans moi."*
   Use sparingly: this one ages fastest with repetition.

## Calibration rules

- Punch at yourself or the situation. Never at the recipient, their team,
  their tooling, or their response time.
- Coffee-machine test: would the user say this line to this person, out
  loud, today? If the relationship is too thin to know, no humor.
- Skip humor entirely: bad news, incident in progress, legal/HR/money
  friction, first contact with a senior stranger, recipient under visible
  pressure, or cross-cultural threads you cannot read.
- Humor never substitutes for the new-element rule: a joke plus the same
  stale ask is still a stale ask.
- Same mechanism twice in one thread reads as a routine. Rotate or stop.
- After the joke, the message must still work if the recipient quotes it to
  their boss. That is the ceiling on edginess.
